\chapter{Introduction}
\label{Section:Introduction}

This thesis studies constrained optimization and online algorithms with
costly domain projections, states that depend on past actions, and limited
feedback.

\section{Contributions}

The summaries below use the models and assumptions stated in each chapter.
In Chapters~\ref{Section:projfree-domain} and~\ref{Section:projfree}, $T$
denotes the query budget for each oracle, including initialization; in the
online problems, it denotes the number of rounds.
Regret measures the controller's cumulative performance shortfall relative
to the stated benchmark.

\subsection{Projection-free nonsmooth optimization}

Chapter~\ref{Section:projfree-domain} develops a method for nonsmooth convex
optimization that couples linear optimization on the original domain with
projected subgradient updates on an auxiliary domain containing it.
Each update uses one linear optimization call, one subgradient call, and
one auxiliary projection. The method returns a feasible point with
objective gap $O(T^{-1/2})$, giving $O(\epsilon^{-2})$ oracle complexity
for an objective gap of at most $\epsilon$.

\subsection{Projection-free optimization with functional constraints}

Chapter~\ref{Section:projfree} addresses additional convex functional
constraints. The returned point belongs to the original domain and has an
$O(T^{-1/2})$ upper bound on objective gap. If an optimal Lagrange multiplier
exists, the constraint-violation norm has the same rate. The constant in the
constraint-violation bound depends on the multiplier's norm, but the algorithm
chooses its parameters without knowing the multiplier.

\subsection{Online allocation with stochastic energy arrivals}

Chapter~\ref{Section:ChapEnergyharvest} separates each allocation among $n$
subsystems into total energy usage and allocation proportions. The controller
updates total energy usage using cost subgradients and a battery-dependent
price that discourages spending when the battery is low. Entropic mirror
descent updates each subsystem's share of that usage. The controller respects
available energy in every slot and
achieves $O(\sqrt{T(1+\log n)})$ expected regret with sufficient battery
capacity $O(\sqrt T)$. The benchmark is the best fixed nonnegative allocation
whose total usage does not exceed the mean energy arrival. The dependence on the number of subsystems
improves from $O(\sqrt n)$ in Yu and Neely~\cite{Yu2019} to
$O(\sqrt{\log n})$.

\subsection{Online control of scalar storage}

Chapter~\ref{Section:ChapControl} introduces simplex disturbance-action
control (SDAC), which allocates fractions of past arrivals to future
withdrawals. Entropic mirror descent updates these fractions, and clipping
keeps withdrawals within the available resource. For adversarial arrivals
and convex costs the controller achieves
$O(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)})$ regret against the best fixed SDAC policy with
memory $H$.

For fixed model parameters, letting the memory length grow logarithmically
with $T$ gives
$O(\sqrt{T\log\log T})$ regret against the best fixed infinite-memory
SDAC policy.

A worst-case expected-regret lower bound of $\Omega(\sqrt T)$ against the
fixed memory-$H$ SDAC class applies to every causal feasible controller,
including randomized controllers.
Thus, the $\sqrt T$ dependence is optimal for fixed memory and model
parameters. It remains unknown whether the regret bound has the best possible
dependence on memory length $H$ and the storage retention coefficient $\alpha$
(Section~\ref{ch4:sec:regret-lower-bound}).

\subsection{Rate adaptation from binary feedback}

Chapter~\ref{Section:ChapRateAdaptation} applies UCB1 to a finite grid of
transmission rates using binary success or failure feedback. The grid spacing
is chosen using a known lower bound $\varepsilon>0$ on the gap between the best
achievable mean offered service and the mean arrival rate. For
independent i.i.d.\ arrival and capacity sequences and an initially empty
queue, the controller achieves time-average expected queue length
$O((1+\log(1/\varepsilon))/\varepsilon^2)$ uniformly over all horizons.
A matching lower bound at a suitable horizon, even with deterministic
arrivals and capacities, shows that the logarithmic factor is necessary
for this UCB1 controller.

\section{Organization of the thesis}

Chapter~\ref{Section:Preliminaries} introduces the shared notation and
online comparisons, then provides the convex-analysis,
Bregman-divergence, and mirror-descent tools used in Chapters~\ref{Section:projfree-domain}--\ref{Section:ChapRateAdaptation}.
Chapter~\ref{Section:Conclusion} summarizes the results and open problems.

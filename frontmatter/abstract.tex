This thesis develops optimization and online learning methods
with explicit performance guarantees for nonsmooth convex optimization,
storage control, and transmission-rate adaptation.

For nonsmooth convex optimization, the methods avoid projections onto the
original domain by using linear optimization there and projecting onto an
auxiliary set. Together with subgradient evaluations, these operations achieve
an $O(T^{-1/2})$ upper bound on the objective gap using at most $T$ calls to
each oracle. With additional convex inequality constraints, the
constraint-violation norm decreases at the same rate whenever an optimal
Lagrange multiplier exists, without requiring the multiplier or a bound on
its norm as input.

For energy allocation across $n\ge2$ subsystems, a controller respecting
available energy achieves $O(\sqrt{T(1+\log n)})$ expected regret over $T$
rounds with sufficient battery capacity $O(\sqrt T)$, independent of $n$.
The benchmark is the best fixed allocation whose total usage does not exceed
the mean energy arrival. The guarantee holds for convex costs revealed after
allocation and bounded i.i.d. arrivals, under the chapter's boundedness and
information assumptions.

For scalar storage control with known leaky linear dynamics, capacity
sufficient to avoid saturation, bounded adversarial arrivals, and adversarial
convex costs, we introduce simplex disturbance-action control (SDAC) policies.
With full cost feedback, a feasible online controller achieves
$O(\sqrt{T\log(H+1)})$ and $O(\sqrt{T\log\log T})$ regret against the best
fixed SDAC policies with memory $H\ge1$ and infinite memory, respectively.
A worst-case expected-regret lower bound of $\Omega(\sqrt T)$ for every
causal feasible controller establishes optimal horizon dependence for fixed
finite memory.

For a communication queue with independent i.i.d. arrival and
channel-capacity sequences in $[0,1]$, a transmitter chooses rates from a
continuous interval and learns their mean offered service from binary
acknowledgments. Given a known positive lower bound $\varepsilon$ on the
difference between the best achievable mean offered service and the mean
arrival rate, UCB1 on a finite rate grid achieves time-average expected queue
length $O((1+\log(1/\varepsilon))/\varepsilon^2)$, uniformly over all horizons.
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the original joint paper gives a worst-case
$\Omega(\varepsilon^{-2})$ lower bound for every causal policy, while a
matching lower bound for this UCB1 controller shows that its logarithmic
factor is unavoidable.
